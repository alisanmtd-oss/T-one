# Trade, quotation, and compliance checklist

Last official-source review: 2026-07-18. These links are time-sensitive pointers, not cached legal conclusions.

## Apparel quotation inputs

- verified product and variant/specification;
- decoration/private-label/packaging requirements;
- quantity by color and size;
- available inventory or production capacity and evidence time;
- destination city/postal code/country;
- shipment mode, freight basis, and delivery assumption;
- currency, unit cost source, margin floor, sample charge, MOQ, validity period;
- payment method and milestones approved by the owner;
- Incoterm and named place, if used;
- tax, duty, customs, certification, and labeling unknowns called out rather than guessed.

## Equipment quotation inputs

Require the apparel inputs that apply plus verified model/configuration, application fit, throughput assumptions, included accessories, consumables, installation, training, warranty, spare parts, site requirements, packaging, gross/net weight, HS/ECCN or other classification evidence where applicable, destination compliance, freight/insurance, commissioning/acceptance criteria, and after-sales coverage.

Do not compute ROI without a versioned formula and evidenced inputs for utilization, labor, yield/waste, consumables, maintenance, depreciation, financing, taxes/duties, and downtime.

## Current official checks before commitment

- Incoterms: use the current ICC Incoterms 2020 checklist and specify the rule plus named place; do not use a three-letter term alone. Source: <https://library.iccwbo.org/content/clp/Others/incoterms_2020_checklist_2024-update.pdf>
- Restricted parties: screen relevant parties against current official lists; the U.S. Consolidated Screening List is one official source, not a universal clearance. Source: <https://www.trade.gov/consolidated-screening-list>
- U.S. export controls when applicable: verify current EAR scope, classification, destination, end user, end use, license requirements, and recordkeeping with BIS/current official regulations. Sources: <https://www.bis.gov/regulations/ear> and <https://www.bis.gov/licensing/end-user-guidance/denied-persons-list-dpl>
- U.S. commercial email: FTC guidance states CAN-SPAM also covers B2B commercial email and requires accurate headers/subjects, identification, postal address, opt-out, and timely suppression. Source: <https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business>
- EU personal data: verify the current GDPR/ePrivacy and destination-country direct-marketing rules before processing or contacting a person. Start with the European Commission legal framework: <https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/legal-framework-eu-data-protection_en>

For other origin/destination countries, product classes, or channels, retrieve the current official customs, sanctions, privacy, marketing, tax, safety, certification, and import rules. Escalate to qualified legal/customs/compliance review when applicability is uncertain.
